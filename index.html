Enhancing non-expert user demonstrations with the geodesic heat flow

Hugo Tadashi M. Kussaba1, Xin Ma2, Rafael I. Cabral Muchacho3,
Riddhiman Laha4, Seth Hutchinson4, Luis Figueredo5
1University of Brasilia, 2Technical University of Munich, 3KTH Royal Institute of Technology, 4Northeastern University, 5University of Nottingham
Paper Code Video

Abstract

LfD (Learning from Demonstration) enables robots to learn movements from human demonstrations and generalize these movements to complete a given task. However, differences in physical embodiment imply that humans often perform actions differently from how a robot would execute the same task, which can reduce the quality of demonstrations collected on robotic platforms. Since the performance of LfD methods strongly depends on the quality of the demonstrations, obtaining near-optimal examples remains challenging, even when demonstrations are provided by experts. This paper addresses the issue of enhancing demonstration quality while preserving the original intention of the user. To achieve this, we propose the application of the geodesic heat flow to transform the originally demonstrated movement, recorded in joint space, into a geodesic path in the robot's configuration space. Our approach captures the underlying geometry of the configuration manifold through a Riemannian formulation. In particular, when the robot inertia matrix is used as the Riemannian metric, the resulting trajectories naturally reflect the system dynamics, leading to smoother motions with lower kinetic energy. Experiments on a real robotic system validate the effectiveness of the proposed method.
